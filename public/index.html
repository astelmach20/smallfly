    What is this?

    A tiny generative-agents-style town, except the residents aren't LLMs — each fly is a leaky-integrate-and-fire simulation of a 30,000-neuron, 1.5-million-synapse slice of the real MaleCNS fruit-fly connectome (smell → antennal lobe → lateral horn + mushroom body → descending neurons → leg/wing/proboscis motor neurons).

    Odors in the town (food, other flies, water) become currents on the fly's actual olfactory receptor neurons, split by antenna side, and the whole slice runs at 1 ms resolution. Steering direction is read from the left/right spike-rate contrast of those simulated receptor neurons (the bilateral comparison real flies use); vigor comes from the leg and wing motor-neuron pools downstream, and the proboscis motor pool decides when a fly on food actually eats. Each fly keeps a memory stream of what happened to it and reflects on its day at dawn.

    What we learned building it: in this simple LIF model, left/right odor information does not survive to the descending neurons — ORNs project to both antennal lobes and the network saturates — so a data-driven decoder over descending neurons ended up fitting noise. We say so rather than fake it.

    Honest caveats: the wiring is measured; the neuron model, synaptic scaling, sensory mapping and motor readout are engineering choices. Nothing here is a validated digital fly. It is a connectome-shaped toy with a town around it.
